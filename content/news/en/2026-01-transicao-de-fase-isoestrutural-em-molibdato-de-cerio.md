---
title: "LaFiM observes a rare phase transition in cerium molybdate at high temperatures"
excerpt: "An article published in ACS Omega reports an isostructural phase transition in Ce₂(MoO₄)₃, a phenomenon rarely described in the literature, investigated by in situ X-ray diffraction and Raman scattering."
---

The article "High-Temperature Isostructural Phase Transition in Ce₂(MoO₄)₃: A Rare Phenomenon Investigated through X-ray Diffraction and Raman Scattering" was published in ACS Omega, from the American Chemical Society (volume 11, issue 2, 2026). The first author is Zeyna dos Santos Viegas, a master's student at LaFiM, and the author list includes the laboratory's coordinator, Prof. Dr. João Victor Barbosa Moura.

Cerium molybdate, Ce₂(MoO₄)₃, is a rare-earth compound with applications in photocatalysis, corrosion inhibition, self-healing protective coatings, and antiviral and antibacterial activity. Even so, its behaviour at extreme temperatures had remained largely unexplored.

The material was obtained by hydrothermal synthesis and characterized by X-ray diffraction, UV-Vis diffuse reflectance spectroscopy, scanning electron microscopy and Raman spectroscopy. The team then followed the sample in situ over a wide temperature range: from 13 K to 973 K (about −260 °C to 700 °C) by X-ray diffraction, and from 293 K to 998 K by Raman scattering.

Rietveld refinement showed that the structure remains stable at low temperatures, but that the lattice parameters, microstrain and crystallite size start to behave anomalously above 583 K. Above 848 K, a new band appears in the Raman spectrum at 452 cm⁻¹. The authors attribute these anomalies to an isostructural phase transition, in which the material changes its properties without changing the symmetry of its crystal structure, one of the rarest types of phase transition reported in the literature.

The results broaden the understanding of the physical properties of scheelite-type compounds and highlight the importance of investigating uncommon phenomena for the development of new materials.

The work brings together researchers from the Department of Physics at UFMA, the Federal University of Piauí (UFPI) and the Federal University of Ceará (UFC).

Reference: Z. S. Viegas, A. S. de Menezes, C. Luz-Lima, P. T. C. Freire, C. C. Santos and J. V. B. Moura. ACS Omega 11(2), 2455–2468 (2026). DOI: 10.1021/acsomega.5c03153
