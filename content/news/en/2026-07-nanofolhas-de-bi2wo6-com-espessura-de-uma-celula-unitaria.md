---
title: "ACS Omega article investigates single-unit-cell-thick Bi₂WO₆ nanosheets"
excerpt: "A study with two LaFiM PhD students among its authors correlates morphology, adhesion and surface potential in two-dimensional bismuth tungstate nanosheets, with potential for photocatalysis and nanoelectronics."
---

The article "Hydrothermal Synthesis and Nanoscale Characterization of Single-Unit-Cell Bi₂WO₆ Nanosheets: Correlation between Morphology, Adhesion, and Surface Potential" was published in ACS Omega, from the American Chemical Society (volume 11, issue 28, 2026). The first author is Charles Duarte Almeida, a PhD student at LaFiM, and the author list also includes PhD student Madson Emanuel Vieira Mendonça and the laboratory's coordinator, Prof. Dr. João Victor Barbosa Moura.

The study addresses bismuth tungstate (Bi₂WO₆) nanosheets as thin as a single unit cell of the crystal, obtained by hydrothermal synthesis. X-ray diffraction, Raman spectroscopy and transmission electron microscopy confirmed the formation of a polycrystalline two-dimensional material. Atomic force microscopy revealed uniform nanosheets with an average thickness of about 1.42 nm.

The team also analysed the mechanical response of the surface at the nanoscale. Increasing the force applied by the microscope tip from 1 to 5 nN irreversibly deforms pre-existing surface pores, while higher oscillation frequencies, from 2 to 4 kHz, cause further topographic changes. This indicates that surface stability depends on frequency.

Using Kelvin probe force microscopy (KPFM), the researchers mapped the surface potential of the nanosheets and found a pronounced spatial variation: the work function is lower at the centre and higher at the edges. This centre-edge contrast establishes a direct link between morphology, adhesion and local surface potential, and helps explain how charge is distributed in the material.

According to the authors, this nanoscale heterogeneity governs surface reactivity and reinforces the potential of Bi₂WO₆ nanosheets for photocatalysis and nanoelectronics.

The work brings together researchers from the Graduate Program in Physics and the Laboratory of Biophysics and Nanosystems at UFMA and the Department of Physics at the Federal University of Pernambuco (UFPE).

Reference: C. D. Almeida, B. S. Batista, M. E. V. Mendonça, D. M. de Oliveira, E. Padrón Hernández, C. C. dos Santos, A. S. de Menezes, L. M. R. Alencar and J. V. B. Moura. ACS Omega 11(28), 42909–42922 (2026). DOI: 10.1021/acsomega.6c05132
